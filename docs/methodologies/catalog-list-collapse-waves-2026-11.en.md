# Catalogue list collapse waves (2026-11): method note

Status: version 0.8, 8 November 2026, about 09:45 UTC, after the pre-publication refresh to 8 November 09:00 UTC (154 reads in window A, 12 collapses; every positive control passed; the window test reproduced the numbers computed in advance on 6 November). Earlier versions are listed under Last validated.

## What this measures

Whether the Bazaar catalogue (the public x402 discovery list, read every six hours at 03:00, 09:00, 15:00 and 21:00 UTC) removes large sellers in waves, meaning several hosts with different payment addresses (`payTo`) on the same day, or separately. It measures only membership of the list at the moments of reading. It does not measure whether endpoints are reachable, what was paid, or why an entry left.

## Definitions

- Host = the network location of `resource_url` (lower case, `www.` removed, port kept). D = the number of distinct `resource_url` values of a host in one read.
- Population of a window = hosts whose maximum D in the window is 200 or more.
- Collapse = the first read with D below 1% of the host's last value of 200 or more before it (within the window). A host can collapse again only after D is back at 200 or more.
- Series = the number of consecutive reads with D below 1%, the collapse read included. Return = D at 1% or more of the reference within 72 hours. Truncated = no return, with less than 72 hours left to the end of the window. Flicker = D at 1% or more of the reference already in the next read.
- Incomplete read = rows fetched divided by the total reported by the API below 0.99, or duplicates above 1% of rows. From 4 November 2026 the crawler records this itself in a status line per read.
- Window A = from the first October read (1 October 2026 03:00 UTC) to the end of the measured window, without gaps; references from before 1 October are not counted. Separate period = September 2026 (1 September 03:00 UTC to 30 September 21:00 UTC), counted on its own.

- Private: my own reads of the public Bazaar discovery API every six hours (03/09/15/21 UTC) since 25 April 2026, stored in my own SQLite database and opened read-only (`mode=ro` and `PRAGMA query_only=1`); the crawler log (summary line, fetched-row count, total reported by the API) and the read-status files (completeness of each read). ## Limits

- Hosts below 200 routes are outside the population: a small host's collapse is invisible to the rule.
- Reads every six hours: an absence shorter than the gap between reads is invisible; the time of a collapse is the time of the read, not of the event.
- "Different `payTo`" means disjoint sets of a host's `payTo` values; hosts sharing even one `payTo` (for example three hosts on 31 October whose entries share a `payTo`) do not make a concentration day. A shared `payTo` is an observable, not a statement about who runs the hosts.
- The list order comes from offset pagination of the API; shifts during a read produce duplicates and gaps (hence the 1% duplicate threshold). An incomplete read can look like a wave (23 September 21:00 UTC: 98 of 16,604).
- Window A runs from 1 October: a collapse whose reference is from before 1 October is not counted. The reference is the last value of 200 or more, also when that read was incomplete (C1's second collapse: by definition the reference is 1 November 03:00 UTC, 11,738; descriptively we give 31 October 21:00 UTC, 76,688).
- The out-of-sample stretch is short (8 reads to 5 November 09:00 UTC; more by 8 November 09:00 UTC); 0 collapses does not confirm the absence of waves. The complete read of 5 November 21:00 UTC is C1's third collapse, inside the out-of-sample stretch, so after the refresh the out-of-sample count is not 0.
- Concentration day = UTC CALENDAR day (rule C): a cluster spread across the day boundary is invisible to the rule (fourth way to refute, window test).
- Rule C says nothing about why an entry was removed or whether the resources worked.

## Ways to refute (four; the fourth was executed)

1. Out-of-sample: rule C keeps running on the reads after 2026-11-03T10:29:14Z (checked by `wi20_refresh.sh <END>`, out-of-sample counts). As of the 5 November 21:00 UTC read: one collapse (C1's third, a complete read), 0 concentration days; the count at publication follows the refresh. The window test is applied to the out-of-sample reads in the same way (a companion within 24 hours with disjoint `payTo`). Every out-of-sample figure is given with its number of reads ("n in N reads").
2. A second measuring road: an independent crawler (different page order or different hour) on the same days sees D at 1% or more of the reference where we record a collapse; then our collapses are artefacts of our reading, not of the list. Partial control: fetched/API ratio and duplicates per read.
3. Sensitivity of the definition: if, with a 5% threshold instead of 1%, or a population of 100 or more instead of 200 or more, window A shows two or more concentration days with collapses in complete reads, the result "1 concentration day" depends on the threshold, not on the data. Not run; the thresholds of rule C do not change, this is a robustness test.
4. Day boundary (EXECUTED on 5 November 2026 by an adversarial review stage of my own pipeline, in a fresh context; reproduced the same night with `k14_window_baseline.py`): rule C's concentration day is a UTC calendar day, so two collapses at 21:00 UTC and 03:00 UTC the next day never make one. Test: the same `payTo` criterion in a sliding 24-hour or 12-hour window. Result: the reading "separately, not in waves" FAILS in that form; 10 of 11 collapses have a companion within 24 hours. How much of that is chance depends on the baseline: about 5.2 of 11 if the 11 collapses were spread over the whole window, about 8.7 of 11 if spread over the band in which they fell; within the band, the neighbourhood is what chance would give. Rule C stays in this note as the frozen test with its result (1 concentration day); Weekly #20 reports BOTH tests and BOTH baselines, and says which test was frozen and which came after the data. Limit: with 11 events, one window (1 October to 5 November) does not support "waves confirmed" either; the pair H06 and C1's second collapse (exactly 24.0 hours) shows sensitivity to the window edge (9 of 11 with strictly less than 24 hours).
